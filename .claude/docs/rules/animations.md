# Animation rules

V/AS `animation/` is binding; these are this shell's additions.

- **Every duration, curve parameter and physics constant is a named token in `config/Motion.qml`** (sizes in `Appearance.qml`). Nothing inline except one-off jolt keyframes.
- **Animations are ability components.** An animation is a component you attach to an element; it plays from that element's state (enter, leave, idle, haptic, click, right click, drag) and the host only sets state. New motion is written as an ability; abilities live in `components/` (`.claude/docs/architecture.md` § Abilities). A host never calls `restart()` on its own timeline; a `Behavior` easing a property towards a state value is allowed.
- **Every element has an enter and a leave; the leave is its own, faster animation, never the enter reversed.** Enter/leave of pieces goes through `components/Turn.qml`.
- **Pills and the pop bar mutate** (`.claude/docs/project-notes.md` § Mutation): reshape in place, own enter/leave, animate inner element changes. Mutation timing comes from `Motion.pill`, kept fast (pop 60 ms, spread 110 ms, leave pop 130 ms as of 2026-11-05). Items inside a spreading pill pop in from the centre outwards, never all at once; the outermost plays only after the pill has reached its full length (2026-11-06).
- **One liquid engine** (user, 2026-11-06). No feature gets its own liquid physics. Every spring, bounce and wobble of a liquid body is a character in `Motion.liquid` (`response`, `damping`, `expansion`) played through `utils/Liquid.js`; necks merge and separate through `Motion.liquid.neck`. A new surface (alerts, clipboard, lock, notifications) picks a character or adds one, so the whole desktop shares one physical identity.
- **Shared geometry lives in `utils/Geometry.js`** ✓. Components do not write their own clamps, smoothsteps, mixes or splines; a new geometric primitive is added there with a test.
- **Everything that animates names itself** (user, 2026-11-09). A `Lifecycle` has a `name` its host sets (`label` on `Burst`), a `Mutate` a `label`; a new ability with an enter and a leave exposes `playing` and enlists in `utils/Playing.js`, so the frame debugger (`KP_0`) can say which animation each frame belongs to.
- **Every top-level animation holds** ✓ (user, 2026-11-09): it declares `paused: Motion.isHeld && running` so the frame debugger (`KP_0`) can freeze and step it; animations nested in a `SequentialAnimation` or `ParallelAnimation` follow their group, and animations inside a `Behavior` or `Transition` are exempt (Qt cannot pause them). A `Timer` that sequences motion is a known gap (`quirks.md`).
- **Reduced motion** (`motion.reduced`, V/AS `animation/08-degradation.md`). Every `duration` in `components/` and `modules/` goes through `Motion.pace`, the one precedence function (enforced by `tools/check-rules.py`). Under it transitions are instant, idle ambience (`Rising`, splashes, crests) does not run, content stays (`TypedText` keeps typing, drags keep following the finger), and exits are as instant as enters. Interaction timers are not motion and are not paced (`Motion.reveal.dwell`).
- **Liquid is one material.** Anything that should read as liquid lives in a `LiquidLayer`'s `shapes` (blur + threshold goo) so it necks and fuses with its neighbours. Droplets, splashes and bands are `LiquidShape`s or `Shape`s inside that layer, never separate coloured overlays.
- **Every shell interaction happens and mutates from the taskbar**, so the shell reads as one unified system. The dashboard is the foundation every overlay adapts: the same liquid `Burst` enter and leave out of the taskbar column (search does; settings does since 2026-11-09). A new surface names the taskbar part it is born from as its `origin` (`rules/features.md`).
- **Motion is as strong as the surface's impact, and ready inside its budget** ✓ (brief, 2026-11-10; `rules/features.md`). Nothing appears unanimated, and nothing plays above its `impact` (`ambient`, `glance`, `focus`, `takeover`). The first content of a surface is there within `Motion.budget.ready.<impact>` of the trigger (`tst_features`); bounce, waves and stagger may settle after that but never block input.
- **Overlays absorb the taskbar.** Everything on the taskbar reacts and merges with an overlay that opens over it: its parts merge into one pill that becomes the overlay (dashboard, search and settings: the parts stream to the taskbar column and form a new circle there, fused to the screen edge, which did not exist before, then it explodes along the dashboard away from the edge, its whole root side curving into the edge like the search lobe, bouncing out past the far wall and back, with waves crashing onto its walls). The taskbar comes back when the overlay has left; the dashboard and search instead give the taskbar back out of themselves (user, 2026-11-09): the body collapses into the circle at the taskbar column, the parts stream back out of it to their places, each as a circle that then extends to its size (the task pill), and the taskbar takes over in place at full size with its icons popping in at random. The taskbar is never visible while the overlay is still open around it. **When a window covers the taskbar as an overlay leaves, nothing is given back** (user, 2026-11-10): the body collapses into its circle and the circle shrinks into the edge, no part streams out, and the taskbar does not animate (its clock included); `OverlayWindow.canHandOff` is off and `Burst` decides the hand-off once, when the leave starts.
- **The burst wave skips the screen edges** (user, 2026-11-08). Wherever a body is connected to a screen edge (the root flare at the taskbar edge, search's full-height top and bottom, the wall's flares into the screen), no crest or trough runs there; the wave shows only where the body is free.
- **Overlays have rising bubbles, not idle liquid motion.** While open, bubbles rise off the body to the screen edges (`Rising`); the body itself stays still. The search's profile blob floats free of the body but holds still too (wobble removed, 2026-11-12); it only swells and splashes once as it lands (`Gulp`). It enters out of the circle at the taskbar's middle before the body bursts, arcing through the bottom-left up to its place. On leave its buttons chase it and merge into it while it already arcs up toward the top-left until it reaches the taskbar edge, slides down along it and, at the middle, curves out of the screen through the edge, shrinking to 0.75 as it goes (`Throw`, `Geometry.arcAt`); it does not merge into the activity pill, never straight back to where they came out (user, 2026-11-12).
- **Search is liquid, not fire**, and opens with the dashboard's burst (the earlier two-lobe clash is gone, 2026-11-05).
- **Press feedback** is the `Press` ability: scale down on press over `Motion.press.down` with OutQuad, back over `Motion.stateDuration` with OutBack; depths from `Motion.press`.
- **Every element of a settings tab enters on its own** (user, 2026-11-09). A tab never pops in as one block. Each label, field, button, fader, wheel and hint is a `Piece` (`modules/settings/Piece.qml`, a `Turn`); `Page` finds the pieces in tree order and staggers their enter by `Motion.settings.stagger`, so a new tab or element only has to wrap itself in a `Piece`. Never put a `Turn` on a whole `Page`, and never add a settings element outside a `Piece`. **Every leave of a settings tab fits `Motion.settings.pageSwap`** (user, 2026-11-09): the next tab starts entering once that time has passed, so no leave (piece flip, label reveal, outline, wipe, cell, untype) may run longer. Leave tokens in `Motion.settings` are `pageSwap` or capped by it, and a `TypedText` in a tab sets `leaveBudget: Motion.settings.pageSwap`. A piece with its own enter (a border trace, a bar sweep, a wipe) sets `isFlipping: false` and starts it once the piece's `presence` rises, so it never flips in as well (user, 2026-11-09).
